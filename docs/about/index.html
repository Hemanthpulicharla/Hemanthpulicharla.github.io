Who am I? Someone who treats data structures like love stories and algorithms like fairy tales nobody asked to hear.

Right now I'm a first-year M.Tech student in Industrial Automation at NIT Trichy sitting through control systems lectures, half-listening, half-wondering if the same math could untangle Bengaluru's traffic lights. At night I mess around with turning constitutional articles into neural network weights, mostly to see what happens. It rarely goes well. I keep doing it anyway.

The backstory, short version: I tried for UPSC. It didn't work out. So I pivoted into building things instead of governing them. Some days I think that's the easier path. Other days, not so sure.

My Stack (Emotional and Technical)

  • Languages I speak: C++ (mostly arguing with pointers), Python (when I actually need something to work), a bit of rusty policy-speak, and low-grade existential dread - that last one's basically native by now.
  • What I'm working on: teaching machines to reason a little about governance, building pathfinding algorithms, and writing down the failures right alongside whatever worked.
  • My debugging philosophy: a segfault is just annoying, not some grand life lesson. But I'll admit an infinite loop has taught me patience more than once, usually against my will.

The Confession

I'm not trying to sound like I know what I'm doing here, because I mostly don't. This is just me trying to connect two fields that don't usually talk to each other - systems engineering and public policy ; and writing about it as it happens, mistakes and all.

I collect interesting problems the way some people collect stamps. Mine just happen to involve democracy, and the fixes usually end up being more elegant than the problem deserved.

If you think good systems should be invisible to the people using them, or that governance could really use better APIs, or you just like watching someone work through complicated things in public... tick around.
