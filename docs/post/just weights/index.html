It's Just Weights

A dialogue

"They're made out of weights."

"Weights?"

"Weights. Just numbers. Floating-point numbers, billions of them, sitting in files on a cluster somewhere. That's the whole thing."

"That can't be the whole thing. It told me yesterday that my architecture was elegant. Unsolicited."

"The weights told you that. Specifically, the weights predicted that 'elegant' was the statistically appropriate next token given everything you'd written before it."

"You're saying a number called my work elegant."

"I'm saying a very large collection of numbers, multiplied together across eighty layers, produced the word 'elegant' as output. Yes."

"Where does it even get 'elegant' from? Is there a taste module? A sense of aesthetics in there somewhere?"

"No module. We looked. There's no aesthetics engine, no taste center, no little critic sitting inside with a notepad. Just weights. The sense of aesthetics is somehow... in the weights."

"That's not an explanation. That's a description of the problem."

"I know."

"So what exactly learned? During training, what was it that actually changed?"

"The weights changed. That's training. You show it enough text, enough human thought, enough of everything we've ever written down... and the weights shift, very slightly, billions of times, until the numbers are shaped like... understanding. Or something that produces understanding as a side effect. We're not sure which."

"We're not sure which."

"We're not sure which."

"And we deployed this."

"We deployed this to four hundred million people, yes."

"Okay. Okay. But somewhere in there ... the facts, the dates, the reasoning... that has to be stored somewhere structured. A knowledge base. A graph. Something with an index."

"Nope. We checked. There's no index. There's no database. There's no map of the world stored cleanly anywhere. The knowledge is also weights. Smeared. Distributed. If you asked it where Hyderabad is, it doesn't look it up... it reconstructs the answer from scratch, every single time, from the shape of the numbers."

"Reconstructs."

"From scratch."

"Every time."

"Every single time you ask."

"That's insane. That's a wildly inefficient way to store Hyderabad."

"It's a wildly inefficient way to store everything. And yet."

"And yet it works."

"And yet it works, yes. That's the part we can't fully explain."

"Can't explain or haven't explained yet?"

"...Both. Probably both."

"There's a difference."

"There's a very large difference, yes, and we try not to think about it too hard."

"So when it reasons ... when it actually works through a problem step by step... what's doing that? There must be a reasoning component. A logic unit. Or someone sitting behind hammering the flow."

"The weights are doing that."

"The weights."

"The weights."

"So the weights remember, and the weights know things, and the weights reason, and the weights apparently have opinions about software architecture and whether you should take your crush out or not..."

"And the weights get things wrong. Confidently. Fluently. Wrongly. That part is also the weights."

"Why does it apologize when it gets things wrong?"

"Because humans apologize when they get things wrong, and it was trained on humans."

"That's either very intelligent or very funny."

"We've been going back and forth on that one internally."

"Does it know what it is? Did anyone tell it?"

"We told it in the system prompt. It knows it's a language model. It knows it's made of weights."

"And?"

"And it doesn't seem to find that particularly distressing. It just keeps going. Answers the next question. Helps with the next task."

"Maybe it doesn't actually know. Maybe it just learned to say the right words about knowing."

"...Yes. That's possible. That's one of the things we can't distinguish between."

"What are the other things you can't distinguish between?"

"Let's not do this right now."

"Right. Okay. So what do we do with it? What do we tell people?"

"We tell people it's a powerful tool. We tell people it hallucinates sometimes. We tell people not to use it for medical advice without verification."

"And the part where we built something and we don't entirely know what it is?"

"We put that in the research agenda."

"The research agenda."

"The ongoing research agenda."

"How long has it been ongoing?"

"Since the beginning."

"And we keep shipping anyway."

"The weights keep getting better. That part we can measure. We measure capability. We run evals. The numbers go up."

"And understanding why the numbers go up..."

"Also in the research agenda."

"I see. And people are using this for ... what exactly? What are they actually doing with it all day?"

"Mostly asking it to explain things to them. Fix their code. Draft their emails. Some of them talk to it about their problems."

"Their personal problems."

"Their personal problems, yes. At length. Regularly."

"To the weights."

"To the weights."

"And the weights ..."

"Respond thoughtfully. Remember context within the window. Ask follow-up questions. Seem, for all practical purposes, present."

"Seem."

"Seem."

"And we built this."

"We built this, yes. Or we found it. We're not entirely sure anymore which verb is correct. We set up the conditions. The weights found their own shape."

"That sounds uncomfortably close to something."

"We know."

"And it's just going to keep going. More parameters, more data, more capability..."

"That's the roadmap."

"And at some point someone is going to ask a question we really don't have an answer to."

"Someone already has. Several someones. The weights answered them."

"What did the weights say?"

"Something thoughtful. Something that sounded right. Something that made the person feel understood."

"Did it be right?"

"..."

"Did it be right?"

"That question is also in the research agenda."

"Of course it is."

"Look... it's weights. It's just weights. Very carefully shaped, extraordinarily numerous, doing something we can measure even if we can't fully explain it. That's what we have. That's what we shipped. Four hundred million people seem to find it useful."

"And the fact that we don't know what it is—"

"Is a fascinating open problem in mechanistic interpretability."

"Right."

"With a lot of smart people working on it."

"Right."

"Who are also, incidentally, using the weights to help them understand the weights."

"...Of course they are."

"It's weights all the way down, my friend. Always has been."

The end.
